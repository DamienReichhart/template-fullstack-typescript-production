# A Video keeps one visually lossless H.264 file, and nothing else

Phones record HEVC, often 10-bit HDR, which many Android phones, Firefox and in-app browsers cannot decode: a Video is therefore played from a single H.264 High, 8-bit 4:2:0, SDR BT.709 MP4 with AAC audio, the one format every phone decodes in hardware. Mathematically lossless H.264 (`-qp 0`, High 4:4:4 Predictive) plays nowhere, so the encode is visually lossless instead: CRF 16, preset `slow`. HDR is tone mapped to SDR (Hable), rotation is applied to the pixels, AAC audio is copied bit for bit (anything else becomes 256 kb/s AAC), variable frame rates keep their timestamps, and every metadata atom (the recording's location included) is dropped. The Source is deleted once the Video is Ready or Failed: a settled Video keeps at most its Playable file. That is irreversible, and the price is accepted: a better encoder or a fixed bug cannot re-encode past Videos, and a Video whose Transcoding failed for good must be uploaded again. To keep a temporary failure from destroying a Source, it is only deleted after the last retry.
